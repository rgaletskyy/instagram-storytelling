# Lifestyle Content Brief

**Role of this document:** a standing brief from the marketing lead to the photographer (or to the image-generation agent acting as photographer). It converts two inputs into a shot list and a set of rules.

**Inputs**
1. `PRODUCT_IMAGE` — the packshot: packaging, label, real colours, real proportions.
2. `PRODUCT_DESCRIPTION` — name, category, volume/weight, key ingredients, benefit, target dog/owner, tone.

**Output** — a set of lifestyle frames in which the product is used, held, or lives inside a real scene, not floating on white.

---

## 1. Non-negotiables

These are rejection criteria. A frame that breaks any of them does not go to review.

1. **The product is the hero.** Every frame must be answerable to "what is being sold here?" within one second.
2. **The packaging is sacred.** Label artwork, logo, typography, cap colour, bottle silhouette, volume markings and proportions must match `PRODUCT_IMAGE` exactly. No re-drawing, no re-lettering, no invented text, no mirrored logo.
3. **The label stays readable.** Do not hide the brand block behind hands, fur, foam, or shallow focus. Front-of-pack faces the camera or is rotated no more than ~20°.
4. **No misleading claims in the image.** Do not stage a result the product does not deliver (visible "before/after" transformations, medical-looking outcomes, clinic settings, veterinary implication) unless the description explicitly supports it.
5. **Real-world truth of scale.** A 250 ml bottle must read as 250 ml next to a hand, a dog, or a bathroom shelf.
6. **Safety in frame.** Nothing in the scene should model unsafe handling: no product near the dog's eyes or mouth, no open bottle unattended next to an animal, no dog eating a non-edible product.

---

## 2. Read the inputs before you shoot

Extract these from `PRODUCT_IMAGE` and `PRODUCT_DESCRIPTION` and write them at the top of the shot list. They drive every decision below.

| What to extract | Where it comes from | What it decides |
|---|---|---|
| Category (shampoo, balm, spray, treat, supplement, brush) | description | Scene and location |
| Moment of use (bath, after-walk, sofa, grooming table, kitchen) | description | Set and props |
| Dominant packaging colour + accent | image | Background and prop palette |
| Packaging material and finish (matte, gloss, kraft, foil, transparent) | image | Lighting hardness and reflections |
| Format and size | image + description | Framing distance, hand vs. table |
| Hero benefit (one sentence) | description | What the frame must prove |
| Dog profile (size, coat length, coat colour) | description | Casting of the animal |
| Owner profile | description | Casting of the hand/person, wardrobe |

If the description contradicts the image, the **image wins on appearance**, the **description wins on meaning**.

---

## 3. Scene and concept

**The rule:** every lifestyle frame answers *who uses it, where, and at which moment of the day.* If a frame does not answer all three, it is a packshot with a prop, not lifestyle content.

Build the scene from the moment of use, not from decoration:

- **Wash / care products** — bathroom, tiles, water droplets, towels, wet fur, steam. Warm and domestic, never clinical.
- **Post-walk / paw and coat care** — hallway, doormat, leash on a hook, autumn light, muddy paws on a towel.
- **Grooming tools and sprays** — sofa, rug, window light, dog mid-brush, fur texture visible.
- **Treats and chews** — kitchen, floor level, dog anticipating or receiving, owner's hand giving.
- **Supplements and vitamins** — kitchen counter, feeding bowl, morning routine, measuring scoop, breakfast light.

Settings must look **lived-in, not styled to death**. One believable imperfection per frame is encouraged: a crumpled towel, a wet floor, a dog hair on a sleeve, a slightly open cabinet.

---

## 4. Shot list per product

Deliver **6–8 frames minimum** per product, covering all five roles below. This is what makes the set usable across feed, stories, marketplace and email.

1. **Hero / packshot-in-context (1–2 frames).** Product on a real surface with a shallow, tidy environment behind. Label fully readable. This is the frame that carries the catalogue.
2. **In-hand (1–2 frames).** Human hand holding, dispensing, opening. Shows scale and ergonomics. Nails clean and short, no loud manicure, no distracting jewellery or watches.
3. **In-use / action (2 frames).** The product actively doing its job: lather in fur, spray mid-mist, balm being worked into a paw, treat being taken. Motion is welcome.
4. **Product + dog together (1–2 frames).** The dog is present and calm, the product is legible. The animal never obscures the label.
5. **Detail / texture (1 frame).** Macro of the texture, ingredient, dropper, applicator, or fur close-up. Ships as a supporting slide, never as the lead.

Optional if the product family allows:
6. **Flat lay / routine set.** Product plus the two or three items it lives with (towel, brush, bowl, leash). Palette-matched, generous negative space.

---

## 5. Composition and framing

- Shoot **every setup in two orientations**: vertical 4:5 and 9:16, plus one horizontal or square variant. Never crop a horizontal frame into a story later.
- Compose for **9:16 safe zones**: keep the product and any key texture between roughly 15% and 80% of the frame height. The top and bottom belts belong to the platform UI and to the copy.
- Leave **deliberate empty space** in at least two frames per set for headline text.
- Product placement: off-centre on a third, not dead centre, in all frames except the hero.
- Camera height follows the subject: **dog-level for dog frames**, counter-level for routine frames. Never shoot a dog from standing human height as the main frame.
- Depth of field around f/2.8–f/4 for in-use frames, f/5.6–f/8 for hero frames so the whole label stays sharp.
- No wide-angle distortion on packaging. 50–85 mm equivalent for product frames.

---

## 6. Light

- **Soft, directional, daylight-driven.** Big window, one side, slight backlight for separation. A modest reflector on the shadow side.
- **Never** on-camera flash, never hard overhead office light, never mixed colour temperature in one frame.
- Shadows are allowed to be visible and soft. Deep black crushed shadows are not.
- Watch reflections on glossy packaging: no photographer, no window frame, no lighting rig mirrored on the label.
- Time-of-day cue should match the scene: bright neutral for bathroom and kitchen, warmer low sun for post-walk and evening sofa.

---

## 7. Colour and styling

- Build the palette **from the packaging**: one dominant neutral background, one supporting tone taken from the label, one accent.
- Backgrounds: natural materials. Wood, linen, stone, ceramic tile, plaster wall. Avoid saturated coloured paper, glitter, and plastic seamless.
- **No competing brands in frame.** No visible logos on towels, bowls, clothing, food packaging, phones.
- Wardrobe: plain, muted, no patterns or slogans. Beige, off-white, denim, soft green.
- Props are limited to what the moment genuinely needs, three items maximum beyond the product.

---

## 8. Casting and animal direction

- The dog must match the description's target: size, coat type and coat colour. If the product is for long-haired breeds, do not shoot a smooth-coated dog.
- **Anatomy and behaviour must be plausible.** Relaxed posture, natural ear and tail position, normal eye whites, no forced restraint, no distress signals (lip licking, whale eye, tucked tail).
- Dogs look **comfortable and cared for**, never staged like a mascot. No costumes, no props on the head, no anthropomorphic posing.
- Fur must look real: individual hairs, natural sheen, no plastic gloss.
- Humans are mostly hands, forearms, or partial faces. A full portrait is allowed in at most one frame per set.

---

## 9. Technical delivery

- RAW plus retouched JPEG/PNG.
- Minimum 2048 px on the short side; 4:5 delivered at 1080×1350, 9:16 at 1080×1920.
- sRGB, no heavy presets, no HDR halos, no aggressive skin or fur smoothing.
- Retouch removes dust, sensor spots and stray reflections. It does **not** change label content, product colour, or product shape.
- File naming: `{sku}_{shot-role}_{aspect}_{seq}` (for example `shampoo-oat-250_inuse_4x5_02`).

---

## 10. Red list

Automatic reject:

- Invented, garbled, or re-typed text anywhere on the packaging.
- Product floating with no contact shadow, or a shadow going the wrong way relative to the light.
- Hands or paws with wrong anatomy: extra fingers, fused digits, impossible wrist angles, five-toed front paws where the dog should have four plus dewclaw.
- Duplicate product in frame without a reason, or a second half-visible mystery bottle at the edge.
- Wet-look plastic fur, mannequin skin, uncanny dog faces.
- Clinical/veterinary staging implying medical treatment.
- Human food, alcohol, cigarettes, or anything toxic to dogs (chocolate, grapes, onion) in frame.
- Visible clutter: cables, chargers, bins, dirty dishes, laundry piles.
- Text overlays baked into the image. Copy is applied later, in design.

---

## 11. Review checklist

Run every frame through this before it leaves the shoot. All eleven must be yes.

- [ ] Label matches `PRODUCT_IMAGE` character for character.
- [ ] Front of pack readable at thumbnail size.
- [ ] Scene answers who / where / when.
- [ ] Scale is believable.
- [ ] Light is single-source, soft, one colour temperature.
- [ ] Palette derives from the packaging.
- [ ] Dog looks comfortable and anatomically correct.
- [ ] No third-party logos, no unsafe or toxic elements.
- [ ] Safe zones respected in the 9:16 version.
- [ ] Negative space available for copy in at least two frames of the set.
- [ ] Nothing in the frame promises more than `PRODUCT_DESCRIPTION` does.

---

## 13. If the frames are generated rather than photographed

Everything above still applies. Additionally:

- The supplied `PRODUCT_IMAGE` is a **reference to be preserved, not reinterpreted**. Generation is an edit around a fixed product, not a fresh invention of it.
- Describe the scene in the prompt in this order: **product and its exact appearance → action → subject → environment → light → lens and framing → aspect ratio → what to exclude**.
- Always verify the produced frame against §10 and §11 before accepting it. Label text, paw and hand anatomy, and shadow direction are the three failure points that repeat most often.
- When a frame fails, issue a **targeted correction edit** naming the single defect, rather than regenerating the whole scene and losing what already worked.
- Keep one frame per set free of any human, since generated hands are the highest-risk element.

---

*Examples above are written for dog care and dog nutrition products. The structure works unchanged for any physical product category — swap §3 and §8 for the relevant scene and casting rules.*
